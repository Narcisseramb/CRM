How to register a company in Madagascar — the practical 2026 guide.

Madagascar welcomes foreign entrepreneurs — 100% foreign-owned companies are allowed, and the one-stop registration system has made the process far simpler than its reputation suggests. But between the French-language paperwork, the local administration and the practical realities on the ground, most foreigners lose weeks. This guide, written by a Toamasina-based business facilitator, walks you through the real process.

1. Choose your legal form: SARL or SARLU

For small and medium ventures, two forms cover the vast majority of cases. The SARLU (Société à Responsabilité Limitée Unipersonnelle) is a limited liability company with a single owner — the standard choice for solo founders. The SARL is the same structure with two or more shareholders. Both offer limited liability, can be 100% foreign-owned, and have no meaningful minimum capital barrier in practice — the capital amount is declared in your articles of association and can be modest.

Larger projects sometimes use the SA (société anonyme), and some activities (mining, banking, certain regulated sectors) carry specific licence requirements — worth checking before you start.

2. The registration steps

Step 1 — Reserve your company name

Your proposed name is checked and reserved with the commercial registry to make sure it is unique.

Step 2 — Draft the legal documents

The core document is the statuts (articles of association), in French, defining the company's name, purpose, capital, registered address and manager (gérant). If you are abroad, a power of attorney lets a local representative sign and file on your behalf — this is how most of our foreign clients proceed, without travelling.

Step 3 — File with the one-stop shop (EDBM)

Registration is centralized through the Economic Development Board of Madagascar (EDBM) in Antananarivo, which processes the commercial registry, tax and statistics registrations together.

Step 4 — Receive your identifiers

A registered company receives its RCS number (commercial registry), NIF (tax identification) and STAT (statistics) — together, these are the proof your company legally exists.

Step 5 — After registration

Typical follow-on steps include opening a local bank account, registering for the relevant tax regime, and — depending on your activity — municipal licences. Ongoing obligations (annual tax filings, bookkeeping) apply like anywhere else.

4. The pitfalls foreigners actually hit

Language: every official document is in French, and administrative communication happens in French or Malagasy. The registered address: you need a valid address in Madagascar (domiciliation services solve this). Sector rules: some activities require additional permits — verify before drafting your purpose clause. Bank account opening: requirements vary by bank and are easier with someone locally who knows which branch to walk into. Follow-up: files don't advance by themselves; polite, persistent local follow-up is half the job.

5. Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner own 100% of a Malagasy company?

Yes — full foreign ownership of a SARL/SARLU is permitted.

Do I need to travel to Madagascar?

No. With a power of attorney, a local representative can handle the entire process remotely on your behalf.

How long does it take?

With a complete file, expect days to a few weeks from filing to certificate, depending on the administration's workload.

What do I receive at the end?

Your registration certificate and identifiers (RCS, NIF, STAT) — the legal proof of your company's existence, needed for banking, contracts and invoicing.
